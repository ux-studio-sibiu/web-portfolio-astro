Scroll the page now and it behaves like the old one. The name and blurb stay up through Work history
and fade as the first project arrives. The black rail comes up with "Projects", then "Experiments",
"Skills" and "Contact". The menu button inverts on the black. Over Contact the whole column fades out
and the button goes back to ink. And the two highlights in the blurb, empty since lesson 03, sweep as
you scroll.

None of it uses a library. It's the scroll effect from `ShowcaseLinear`, moved into
`showcase/scroll-sequence.ts`, plus the wheel forwarding in `showcase/forward-wheel.ts`.

## Measure, then react to scroll

The sequence works in two passes.

**`measure()`** runs on load, on resize and whenever the content changes height. It finds the bands
(`[data-section]`) and works out, **in pixels of `scrollTop`**, where things happen:

- `fadeStart → fadeEnd`: the identity fades out, finishing exactly as the handover band's anchor
  crosses the **trigger line** (half the pane's height);
- `outroStart → outroEnd`: the whole column fades out as the last band (Contact) comes up.

**`onScroll()`** runs on every scroll. It only does arithmetic against those numbers, writes a few CSS
custom properties and works out which band owns the title:

| Written | Where | Read by |
|---|---|---|
| `--identity-fade` (1 → 0) | the column | the three parts of the identity, each fading in its own slice (`fixed-column.scss`) |
| `--sweep-px`, `--sweep-over` | the column | `.highlight-on-scroll.pinned` in `globals.scss`: the blurb's two highlights |
| `--column-fade` (1 → 0) | the column **and** the rail | both fade out together over Contact |
| `.is-column-hidden` | the shell | hides the column completely once faded, so it can't be clicked |
| `section`, `lit` | reported to the controller | `render()`: rail title, rail up, button inverted |

All the knobs are **fractions of the pane height**, not pixels. That's the point the original comment
makes: band positions move with viewport size and text reflow, and a fixed 2000px threshold "landed
1382px early at 1280x720".

### Writing on two small boxes, not the shell

The custom properties are written on the **column** and the **rail**, not on the shell around
everything. Custom properties are inherited, so every write re-resolves style for the whole subtree
below the element. From the shell that was 338 nodes and 2.4 ms per scroll event; on these two boxes
it's 0.3 ms. That measurement came from the Next version and still applies, so the port kept it.

### Two attributes a band can set

- **`data-identity-handover`** on Projects: the identity stays up through Work history (which is read
  over the name) and gives way when Projects arrives. Bands before the handover light no rail title.
- **`data-section-anchor`** on the first project entry: a band's box has padding, so its top crosses the
  line before anything visible does. The band nominates the element to time off instead.

Both were set by the bands in lesson 05, and nothing read them until now. That's the `data-*` handoff
from build to script again (G3).

### Report changes, not frames

`onScroll` runs dozens of times a second, but `section` and `lit` change maybe ten times on a full
scroll. `scrollSequence` only calls `onChange` when one of them actually changes, and only then does
the controller update its state and call `render()`. React's `setState` bailed out of equal values for
you. Here it's three lines in `report()`.

## Re-measuring: `ResizeObserver`

Scroll offsets are only right until something reflows: fonts swapping in, a picture decoding, a Work
history entry opening or closing. A window `resize` doesn't fire for any of those, because the content
changed height **inside** a scroller of our own. A `ResizeObserver` on the scroller and on its content
does, and it re-measures. Lesson 10 also hangs `ScrollTrigger.refresh()` off it, through the `onResize`
option that's already in place.

## Wheel forwarding

The fixed column is laid **over** the scroller with `pointer-events: none`, so the wheel falls through
to the pane. But the rail button takes pointer events, and it has no scrollable ancestor: `.pane-scroll`
is its *sibling*. Over it, a wheel event would scroll nothing. `forwardWheel()` catches the wheel there
and adds `deltaY` to the pane's `scrollTop` by hand, converting lines and pages (`deltaMode` 1 and 2)
into pixels.

It needs **`{ passive: false }`**. Wheel listeners are passive by default, and `preventDefault()` on a
passive listener is silently ignored.

## How it was checked

Two things worth knowing about testing scroll code:

1. **A hidden page fires no scroll events.** The first probe ran in a browser tab that wasn't
   visible, and nothing updated, not even `--sweep-px` at the very bottom. The code was fine:
   browsers fire `scroll` during the rendering steps, and a background page doesn't render. The probe
   dispatches `new Event("scroll")` itself after each jump.
2. **Compare with the original, don't eyeball it.** The same probe ran on the Next build and on this one
   at the same viewport (1024 px wide). It sampled `scrollTop` every 250 px, 50 positions from top to
   bottom, recording the identity fade, the column fade, the rail title and the button inversion. **All
   50 rows matched**, and so did the scroll height (12,317 px). Some samples, from both:

| scrollTop | identity | column | rail | button |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3000 | 1.00 | 1.00 | — | |
| 3250 | 0.43 | 1.00 | — | |
| 3500 | 0.00 | 1.00 | Projects | inverted |
| 6500 | 0.00 | 1.00 | Experiments | inverted |
| 11000 | 0.00 | 0.87 | Skills | inverted |
| 11250 | 0.00 | 0.13 | Skills | |
| 11500 | 0.00 | 0.00 | Contact | |

Wheel forwarding was checked by dispatching a 120 px wheel event on the column and a 3-line one on the
rail button: the pane scrolled 168 px (120 + 3 × 16), and both events were `defaultPrevented`.
